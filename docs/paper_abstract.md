# Abstract: A Comprehensive Machine Learning Framework for High-Dimensional Spectral Data Analysis and Material Property Prediction

This paper presents an advanced and multi-faceted machine learning framework designed for the robust analysis of high-dimensional spectral data, with the ultimate goal of accurately predicting critical material properties. The increasing complexity of modern spectroscopic instruments necessitates sophisticated computational approaches to extract meaningful information, handle intrinsic noise, and manage vast feature spaces effectively.

Our methodology integrates state-of-the-art signal processing techniques, including advanced asymmetric least squares baseline correction, adaptive frequency-domain denoising, and experimental wavelet transform methods, to ensure optimal signal integrity. We explore a hybrid modeling strategy combining deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for hierarchical feature learning from raw spectral inputs with highly optimized Least Squares Support Vector Machines (LSSVMs) for their strong generalization capabilities on complex regression tasks.

A core component of this framework is its sophisticated hyperparameter optimization engine, leveraging Bayesian optimization with extensive search spaces for both architectural and algorithmic parameters. This includes a broad exploration of CNN filter configurations, kernel types for LSSVMs, and learning rate schedulers to achieve peak performance. Furthermore, we investigate the potential of ensemble stacking for meta-modeling and advanced data augmentation strategies such as spectral shifting, stretching, and noise injection to enhance model robustness and generalization across diverse conditions.

Finally, the framework incorporates model-agnostic interpretability methods, specifically SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis, to provide transparent insights into the influence of specific spectral regions on property predictions, addressing the black-box nature often associated with complex machine learning models. While designed for broad applicability, this framework is particularly suited for industrial and research contexts where precise material characterization from spectral data is paramount.
